# Costivra Marketing Site Clarity Plan

## Working document — edit this before any front-end work

**Last reviewed:** August 8, 2026
**Purpose:** Make Costivra's public site immediately understandable to the right business owner, controller, CFO, or operations leader without weakening the evidence, approval, privacy, and "verified" standards that make the product credible.
**Implementation status:** The first clarity pass is in progress. This remains an editable plan that breaks the remaining work into small, reviewable chunks. No branch, commit, or deployment is required to use it.

### The standard for every public page

Within about 10 seconds, a qualified visitor should be able to answer:

1. What does Costivra do?
2. Is this meant for a business like mine?
3. What kinds of bills or costs can it review today?
4. What happens if I click the main button?
5. Why might this be worth paying for later?
6. Will Costivra do anything with my information or vendors without my approval?

If a page cannot answer those questions in plain language, it is not clear enough yet.

## Start here: the big conclusion

Kinley's reaction is useful, but it does **not** automatically mean Costivra should be made for every very small business or that the price should be lowered.

She gave us three separate signals:

- The page feels like a serious, complicated system before she understands the simple outcome.
- The name plus the word "scan" can accidentally create a medical-software association.
- She does not yet see enough likely value for a small owner-operated business to justify a paid ongoing tool.

The right response is not "make Costivra cheap for everyone." The right response is:

1. Make the outcome much clearer.
2. State who Costivra is best for, so an owner who is too small can self-qualify without feeling tricked.
3. Show what the free first step actually gives them.
4. Let evidence and a real review demonstrate value before asking them to believe in a large platform.

Costivra should be positioned as a recurring-bill review and cost-control system for businesses with enough recurring vendors, locations, people, or renewal exposure for missed waste to matter. It should not lead with "AI," a dashboard, or a promise of guaranteed savings.

### The repeated pattern

The financially proven category leaders do not make a cold visitor learn their internal system first. They generally do this in order:

1. State a familiar business outcome.
2. Say who it is for.
3. Name a few concrete jobs or problems.
4. Give one next action.
5. Add product proof, trust detail, and technical depth after interest exists.

Kontai is useful because it follows a simple sequence, not because it is proven to be a better business than Costivra.

**Recommended rule:**

- Clearly label each as **Coming soon**, **Planned coverage**, or **Join the early-access list** until it is usable by a customer.
- Do not let a future-category page use language such as "we audit," "we find," or "we recover" as if the service is already available.

A polished future page can build demand. A page that looks like a live service but cannot deliver creates disappointment and hurts trust.

## Principles for the redesign

### 1. Borrow the sequence, not the competitor's site

We should not copy Kontai's wording, visual design, content, or structure. The useful principle is simple:

**Plain outcome first → concrete examples → low-friction next step → proof and detail.**

### 2. Put the literal job before the category language

Avoid making a first-time visitor decode phrases such as:

- Recurring cost intelligence
- Evidence-linked extraction
- Typed facts
- Chain of custody
- Idempotency
- Opportunity engine

Those ideas matter, but they belong lower on the page or on detail pages. Start with language like:

> Review selected business bills for price increases, duplicate charges, unused services, and renewal deadlines.

Then explain that each finding links back to the source document and that the customer decides what happens next.

### 3. Use one primary action per moment

The primary call to action needs a literal promise and a clear next screen. A visitor should never wonder whether a click will:

- Create an account;
- Upload a document;
- Book a sales call;
- Start a paid trial;
- Send information to a vendor; or
- Authorize anything.

Candidate wording to test after the free offer is defined:

- Review 3 bills free
- Upload 3 bills for review
- See what a bill review finds

Avoid using "Scan" as the default noun or verb until testing shows that visitors understand it. This is not a mandate to rename the company.

### 4. Treat fit as a feature

It is healthy for some visitors to realize Costivra is not a fit yet. Plain qualification increases trust and saves sales time.

Candidate fit language:

> Built for businesses managing recurring vendors across people, locations, services, or contracts.

Do not claim a minimum spend, a guaranteed savings amount, or a payback period until Costivra has factual support for it.

### 5. Keep the evidence advantage, but reveal it at the right time

Costivra's differentiation is not a generic dashboard. It is:

- Finding questions worth investigating;
- Showing the source evidence;
- Keeping claims honest when information is missing;
- Requiring a human decision before consequential action; and
- Distinguishing potential value from verified value.

That story should be visible, but it should not be the first thing a cold visitor has to decode.

### 6. Keep category, security, and energy claims strictly true

Do not create copy that implies:

- Guaranteed savings;
- Automatic cancellation or vendor negotiation;
- Autonomous use of customer documents;
- A live future category;
- Legal or regulatory conclusions;
- Automatic energy referral; or
- A verified result before the evidence and method support it.

- Who each solution is for;
- What a typical issue looks like;
- What first document to upload; or
- What Costivra will and will not do.

Future categories can look like active service pages even though Lewis is still building them.

## Proposed layout for live categories

Give each live category the same three plain cues:

| Category | Best for | Common question Costivra can flag | Useful first document |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Software subscriptions | Teams with many tools, seats, renewals, or owners. | Are we paying for duplicate, unused, or changed subscriptions? | Current vendor invoice or subscription record. |
| Telecom and internet | Businesses with locations, lines, devices, circuits, or service changes. | Did a rate, service, line, or fee change? | Current telecom/internet invoice. |
| Commercial-energy review | Businesses with commercial energy bills, contracts, renewal dates, or unclear charges. | Is there a deadline, anomaly, missing data point, or reason for professional review? | Current energy invoice or agreement. |

Only keep rows that are supported by actual product workflows.

## Proposed layout for future categories

For Insurance, Facilities, Merchant, and other planned categories:

1. Use a visible **Planned coverage** or **Coming soon** label beside the headline.
2. Explain the business problem Costivra intends to help with, without claiming it already performs the review.
3. Offer an interest or early-access action instead of the live upload/review CTA.
4. Give Lewis a place to capture prospects who want that category, with clear consent and no promise of a delivery date.
5. Promote a category to live only when its extraction, evidence, review, policy, and customer-facing flow are actually ready.

## Energy guardrail

The energy page must keep the current neutral boundary:

- Costivra can identify questions and prepare evidence.
- The customer chooses whether to export, save, assign an advisor, or request a disclosed UCEP review.
- No automatic lead transfer, supplier selection, quote ranking, or hidden-compensation framing.

## Do not do in this chunk

- Do not reintroduce an unsupported category as a live audit.
- Do not state that Costivra negotiates, cancels, or changes a vendor contract automatically.
- Do not use live-category pages to make unsupported savings estimates.
- Do not promise future-category availability dates until Lewis can support them.

## Acceptance checks

- A visitor can choose their live category in one glance.
- Each live page names a common problem and a useful first document.
- A visitor can tell which categories are live versus planned before they click.
- Every category page accurately reflects its approval boundary.
- Energy choices remain neutral, disclosed, and consent-led.

The marketing styles contain several overlapping selector blocks and repeated overrides for shared elements such as the header, hero, and content hero. # Chunk 9 — Test clarity with real people and measure results

**Status:** [ ] Not started
**Dependency:** Core content chunks are live in a safe preview or test environment.

## Goal

Prove that the simplification helps qualified prospects understand and act, rather than relying on the founder's taste or one person's reaction.

## Who to test with

Use at least five people who resemble the intended first customer:

- Business owners, controllers, finance leads, or operations leads;
- Businesses with recurring vendors, services, locations, or renewal dates;
- At least a few people closer to the intended size or complexity than Kinley's business;
- One or two smaller owners for a useful "not a fit yet" response.

Do not test only friends, technically inclined people, or people who already know Costivra.

## Five-second / ten-second interview script

Show the home page without explaining it. Ask:

1. What do you think this company does?
2. Who do you think it is for?
3. What would happen if you clicked the main button?
4. What kinds of problems do you think it might find?
5. What would make you trust or distrust it?
6. Does the price feel potentially worthwhile, not worthwhile, or too unclear to judge? Why?

Avoid leading the person toward the answer.

## Metrics to record

| Signal | How to record it |
| --- | --- |
| Plain-language comprehension | Could the person describe the product correctly without jargon? |
| Audience fit | Did a likely customer identify themselves as a fit? Did a non-fit visitor understand why? |
| CTA expectation | Did they correctly predict account creation, upload, and expected result? |
| Trust | Did they understand that evidence is shown and approval is required before consequential action? |
| Pricing clarity | Could they explain what makes ongoing monitoring worth evaluating? |
| Conversion behavior | Track the real funnel only after privacy-respecting analytics and event definitions are in place. |

## Success condition

The first version is working when a qualified visitor can explain Costivra in their own words, expects the next step correctly, and can see why a source-backed recurring-bill review might be worth exploring.

Do not call a result "successful" just because a visitor likes the design.
